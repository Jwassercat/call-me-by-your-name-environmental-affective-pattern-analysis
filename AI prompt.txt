How do environmental elements and visual parameters, such as chroma and lighting, convey meaning to the audience in Call Me by Your Name?
First, let us look at the effect of chroma on cinematic mood and character emotion. In one of the film’s iconic scenes, Elio and Oliver swim in an outdoor pool surrounded by enchanting greenery. The pool enables their first bodily contact, where sensory seduction, curiosity, desire, and affection begin to emerge. The scene presents very high chroma, or color intensity, which supports the visual expression of the characters’ emotional vitality.
However, if we reduce the chromatic quality of this scene, the atmosphere changes immediately. The scene is no longer as emotionally cheerful, sensuous, or visually appealing as before. Instead, it begins to look nostalgic, muted, and sad, while the characters seem deprived of their original vitality. This shows how chroma conveys meaning by shaping our visual perception of atmosphere and character emotion.

They walk through the town in the evening, where the dim lighting seems to help Elio conceal his true feelings. If we alter the chroma and lighting of this scene, what we see becomes slightly bizarre. The quiet, melancholic scene and its ambiguous emotional atmosphere turn into something strangely vivid, relaxed, and almost cheerful, contradicting the emotional tension between the characters.

Imagine replacing the soothing outdoor setting and surrounding greenery with concrete walls and ceramic tiles, as if the two characters were having their first flirtatious encounter in an indoor Olympic-style swimming pool. The result feels bizarre. The gentle, nuanced, and sensuous atmosphere is almost completely lost.
Together, these visual experiments show that meaning in film is not produced by characters and plot alone; it is also shaped by color, light, and environment, which guide how viewers feel the emotional world of the story.
